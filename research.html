Job Market Paper

When Primary Care Collapses: Rural Economic Reform and Rising Mortality in China

With Minhee Chae and Xin Meng · Job Market Paper

China’s Household Responsibility System replaced collective farming with household production and substantially raised agricultural productivity. At the same time, dismantling the communes weakened the collective financing and organizational structures that had supported the Cooperative Medical Scheme, village clinics, and barefoot doctors. We ask whether this institutional transition raised mortality by weakening local primary-care capacity.

We construct a new county-by-year panel from more than 3,000 local gazetteers and provincial statistical records, linking HRS timing to mortality, infectious-disease incidence, village-level medical personnel, fiscal spending, and local economic conditions. Exploiting staggered county-level HRS adoption, we estimate difference-in-differences, event-study, and trend-break specifications.

County mortality rises by about 1.6 percent after reform, implying roughly 90,000 excess deaths annually at the national scale. The increase is concentrated in counties with the largest declines in village-doctor availability. Several vaccine-preventable diseases exhibit post-reform reversals in their previous downward trends, while local disease surges become more frequent. The results are not explained by reductions in formal government health expenditure; if anything, post-reform income gains modestly offset the deterioration in health.

Working Papers

Carrots and Sticks: Decoding China’s One-Child Policy with Large Language Models

With Xin Meng and Sen Xue

This paper studies how local governments translated China’s common fertility-control objective into heterogeneous policy instruments. Using large-language-model (LLM)-assisted analysis of county gazetteers, we construct new county-level measures of the types and intensity of monetary and non-monetary rewards and penalties specified in local family-planning policies. This allows us to move beyond a single measure of OCP exposure and examine how the design of local policy tools relates to fertility behavior. When the four types of instruments are considered jointly, non-monetary penalties—primarily administrative sanctions—are most strongly associated with fertility decline, and the relationship is significantly stronger in urban areas than in rural areas.

A new investigation on Land Reform, OCP and Sex Selection in China

With Xin Meng and Sen Xue

Building on the pioneering work of Almond et al. (2019), we revisit the relationship between the Household Responsibility System, the One-Child Policy, and sex selection in rural China. Using an expanded county sample, we reconstruct local exposure to both reforms and distinguish pilot and broader adoption stages. The new measures reveal a different pattern: the effects are concentrated among households exposed to both policies. The findings suggest that economic resources and fertility constraints interacted to intensify households’ sex-selective behavior. We further find evidence that the pilot and broader implementation phases of each reform had differential impacts on fertility outcomes, reflecting evolving mechanisms as the two policies progressed.
